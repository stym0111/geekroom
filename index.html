Part 1

Communication with a dress code

by Member 1

What is formal communication?

Messages that follow official channels, a set structure and a professional tone. Everyone knows who said what, to whom, and why. Best part: it leaves a record, so "I never got told" stops working.

Think of it as texting your friend vs. emailing the principal. 🎓

Which way does the message travel? Tap one 👇

Boss to team. Think notices, circulars, instructions and policy updates.

Part 2

Meetings: before and after

by Member 2

AGENDA: Project check-in

Fri, 10 Nov 2026, 3:00 pm, Room 204
Chair: Member 1. Secretary: Member 2

1. Welcome and apologies (3 min)
2. Minutes of last meeting (5 min)
3. Presentation progress (15 min)
4. Task allocation (10 min)
5. Any Other Business (5 min)
6. Date of next meeting

MINUTES: Project check-in

Held Fri, 10 Nov 2026, 3:00 pm, Room 204
Present: all six. Apologies: none

Item 3. The team reported slides were 70% complete. It was decided that design would be finished by Wednesday.

Action: Member 3 to finish visuals. Deadline: Wed, 15 Nov.

Meeting closed at 3:35 pm. Next meeting: 17 Nov.

  • Send it 2 to 3 days before the meeting
  • Give every item a time slot and a lead person
  • Put the most important items first
  • Finish with "Any Other Business" and the next date

No agenda = a 2-hour chat about snacks. 🍕

  • Send within 24 to 48 hours
  • Use past tense and neutral language
  • Record decisions and actions, not every word
  • Every action needs an owner and a deadline
  • Approved at the next meeting

If it's not in the minutes, it didn't happen. 😉

Part 3

Applications that get a yes

by Member 3
Hook

Name the role and where you found it. "I am writing to apply for the Content Intern role listed on your careers page."

Fit

Match two skills to what they need. "I write for our college magazine and manage its social media."

Proof

One real achievement, with a number. "My article series grew readership by 40%."

Ask

Request the next step and say thanks. "I would welcome the chance to discuss my application."

Request

Say what you want upfront. "I request leave for two days, 12 and 13 November 2026."

Reason

Short and honest. "I need to attend a family wedding out of town."

Cover

Show responsibility. "I will complete my pending assignment before leaving."

Thanks

"Thank you for considering my request."

Do ✅
Address the person by title. Keep it to one page. Attach your résumé or proof. Check every name and date.
Don't ❌
Over-explain. Beg. Use slang. Send the same copy-pasted letter to every company.

Part 4

Notice vs memo

by Member 4

GREENFIELD COLLEGE

NOTICE

4 November 2026

Tech Fest: Volunteers Wanted

Students who wish to volunteer for the Tech Fest on 20 November may register at the Student Council office by 12 November. Seats are limited to 40, first come, first served.

Riya Verma
Secretary, Student Council

MEMORANDUM

To: All Team Leads
From: Anil Kulkarni, HR Manager
Date: 4 November 2026
Subject: Revised Friday working hours

From 10 November, Friday office hours will be 9 am to 4 pm. Please inform your teams by Monday and send any concerns to HR by 8 November.

AK

  • For many people, often the public
  • Lives on a notice board, website or intranet
  • Issuer, "NOTICE", date, heading, body, signature and designation
  • Short. Readers pass by in seconds
  • For specific people inside one organisation
  • To, From, Date, Subject, then straight to the point
  • No "Dear..." and no "Yours sincerely"
  • Initials instead of a full signature

Memory trick: a notice is a billboard, a memo is a note passed across the office.

Part 5

Email makeover

by Member 5
Toprof.sharma@college.example
Subjecthi
hey sir pls check attachment n tell me if its ok. need it fast thx
Toprof.sharma@college.example
SubjectRequest for feedback: CS presentation outline (due Fri 10 Nov)
Dear Prof. Sharma, I hope you are well. I have attached the outline for our Communication Skills presentation. Could you please share your feedback by Thursday so our team can revise it before Friday? Thank you for your time. Regards, Riya Verma CS Section B
  • Vague subject: nobody knows what "hi" is about
  • No greeting, no name, no sign-off
  • Text-speak and a demand, not a request
  • Says "attachment" but forgot to attach it
  • Subject says what, and by when
  • Polite greeting and sign-off
  • One clear ask with a deadline
  • Attachment actually attached

Before you hit send: check To, Cc and Reply All. Use Bcc to protect people's addresses.

Part 6

Technical communication

by Member 6

Explaining complex or specialised information so a specific reader can understand it and act on it.

User manualsReportsProposalsInstructionsFAQsData sheets

Audience-focused, accurate, clear, and visual where it helps. 📊

"What does a Wi-Fi router do?" Pick your reader:

It's like a post office at home. It picks up letters from the internet and hands them to your phone and laptop.
It connects all our devices to the internet. A better one means fewer dropped calls and less downtime for the team.
It routes packets between WAN and LAN, assigns addresses through DHCP, performs NAT and serves as an 802.11 access point.

Same fact, three versions. Your reader decides the words.

Academic vs professional writing

by Member 6
GoalShow understanding and argue a point
ReaderTeachers, examiners, researchers
StructureIntroduction, body, conclusion (and an abstract for papers)
EvidenceCited sources in APA, MLA or IEEE
StyleFormal, objective, longer paragraphs
GoalGet something decided or done
ReaderBusy colleagues, clients, managers
StructureBottom line first, then headings and bullets
EvidenceData, facts and references where needed
StyleShort, direct, action-oriented

Both need: clarity, accuracy and honesty. And never plagiarise: if it's not your idea, cite it. 🙅
